# Error taxonomy + routing (Year 11–12 analytical — IA2 + EA)

Used by `diagnose-errors`. Categories are ordered by leverage — fix higher categories first. Same five categories as the lower bands; the band difference is what counts as "sound". Anchored to the official QCAA English 2025 v1.3 syllabus (© State of Queensland (QCAA) 2026, CC-BY 4.0; `research/official/english-2025-v1.3-syllabus.md`): IA2 ISMG pp. 40–42; EA specifications p. 48. EA is exam-mode analytical — same categories under exam conditions.

| # | Category | What it looks like at Year 11–12 (IA2 / EA) | Routes to |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Argument / point** | No thesis or critical perspective; describes the representation instead of interpreting it; doesn't answer the (unseen) question; in IA2, no focused perspective connecting the two texts. | `check-structure` (Point/thesis) |
| 2 | **Analysis / evidence** | Analyses the representation but never reaches the **cultural assumptions, attitudes, values and beliefs** underpinning it or how the text **invites the audience to take up a position** (caps Knowledge application at "adequate", 5/9); quote-dumping; asserts without textual proof; IA2 evidence drawn from only one of the two required texts. | `check-structure` (E1/E2) |
| 3 | **Structure / cohesion** | Two texts treated as sequential halves instead of synthesised (IA2); paragraphs don't advance the perspective; weak cohesion (devices connect sentences but don't emphasise ideas, ISMG p. 41); genre conventions drift (article reads as a school essay); role of the writer not maintained with the public audience. | `check-structure` (L / essay-level) |
| 4 | **Language / vocabulary** | Informal register for an educated/public audience; flat or repeated analytical verbs; missing senior metalanguage (*aesthetic features, stylistic devices, representation, positioning, cultural assumptions*); imprecise literary terminology (syllabus p. 12 genre note). | `elevate-vocabulary` |
| 5 | **Mechanics** | Grammar, punctuation, spelling errors (only once higher levels are sound). ISMG Textual features still requires grammatically accurate/appropriate structures for the top bands. | `fix-mechanics` |

## Priority rule
Choose the primary issue as the **lowest-numbered category rated "major"**. Rationale: a strong vocabulary can't rescue a response with no critical perspective, so fix the argument first. Mechanics is last — don't polish sentences that have no point.

## Band calibration
- Evaluation of representation + reader positioning is **the floor at this band, not the ceiling** — do not flag its presence as a gap; flag the *missing cultural-assumptions/values/positioning analysis* (category 2) or missing synthesis (category 3) instead.
- An explanation-only paragraph (sound at Year 8, C-level at Year 9–10) is **superficial at this band** — ISMG Knowledge application 3–4/9 territory.
- Informal register remains a **major** category-4 issue: the EA audience is an educated reader with deep understanding of the text, and IA2 targets a public audience.
- For EA-mode work, also check the response actually answers the **unseen question** — a polished pre-prepared essay that dodges the question is a category-1 problem.

## Strong-response case
If nothing is "major" and at most one thing is "minor", route to `give-feedback` with a *stretch* target (e.g. lift one analysis from "effective" to "discerning" by naming the cultural assumption the representation rests on, or sharpen the public-conversation angle of the IA2 perspective) rather than inventing a fault.
