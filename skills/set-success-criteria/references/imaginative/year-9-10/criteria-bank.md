# "I can…" criteria bank (Year 9–10 imaginative)

Reusable, student-friendly phrasings mapped to QCAA Year 9 imaginative capabilities (reaserch.md — Imaginative formats, Year 9 row: structural experimentation, manipulating narrative voice, changing character points of view, playing with tone; Marking Criteria Year 9: purposeful selection and considered experimentation). Adapt to the specific format/voice — don't use verbatim if a more specific version fits.

**Provenance note (Q-001):** Year 10 expectations are derived from the Year 9 elaborations, not verbatim QCAA text.

## Structural experimentation
- I can choose a structure (non-linear, multi-voice, shifting POV) because of what it *does*, not for its own sake.
- I can keep my reader oriented even when my structure jumps around.
- I can plant a detail early and pay it off at the end.
- I can make my ending re-frame the whole piece, not just stop it.

## Voice & tone
- I can hold a distinct narrator voice across the whole piece.
- I can shift tone deliberately — and signal the shift.
- I can change point of view on purpose and make the change clear.
- I can make a monologue voice carry the piece on its own.

## Showing & imagery
- I can show my key moments instead of summarising them.
- I can return to an image so it builds meaning (a motif), not just decorate.
- I can understate an emotion when understatement lands harder.

## Language
- I can choose words that fit my narrator's voice, not just "impressive" words.
- I can make the same event sound different in two different voices.
- I can cut "very" and let the stronger word carry the intensity.

## Response-level (extended / multimodal)
- I can sustain a complication across 600–800 words without repeating myself.
- I can keep voice and thread across instalments of a serialized format.
- I can plan in the 10 minutes of perusal time so experimentation stays controlled.

## Learning intention stems
- "We are learning to experiment with structure while keeping the reader oriented."
- "We are learning to craft and control a narrative voice."
- "We are learning to sustain imagery into meaning."
