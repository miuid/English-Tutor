# "I can…" criteria bank (Year 8 imaginative)

Reusable, student-friendly phrasings mapped to QCAA Year 8 imaginative capabilities (reaserch.md — Imaginative formats, Year 8 row: creative plot development, expanding on character actions; Marking Criteria Year 8: purposeful creation, variation of text structures and language features). Adapt to the specific format/character — don't use verbatim if a more specific version fits.

## Story & tension
- I can give my story one clear complication that matters.
- I can build tension scene by scene instead of listing events.
- I can slow down the most important moment.
- I can make my ending follow from what happened — and cost something.

## Showing & detail
- I can show a feeling through action or detail instead of naming it.
- I can use at least one precise sensory detail in my key scene.
- I can let the reader work out the emotion instead of telling them.

## Character & voice
- I can keep one point of view for the whole piece.
- I can make my character act like the person I've shown the reader.
- I can make my narrator sound like a real, specific voice.

## Vocabulary / language
- I can use precise verbs instead of "went" and "said".
- I can choose adjectives that fit the exact thing, not "nice" or "good".
- I can avoid filler intensifiers like "very" and "really".

## Response-level (short story / memoir / diary)
- I can hook the reader in the first lines.
- I can start a new paragraph when time, place, or speaker changes.
- I can write in the format the task asks for (story, diary, memoir).

## Learning intention stems
- "We are learning to build tension around one clear complication."
- "We are learning to show emotions through concrete detail."
- "We are learning to hold a consistent narrative voice."
