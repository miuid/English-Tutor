# "I can…" criteria bank (Year 9–10 persuasive)

Reusable, student-friendly phrasings mapped to QCAA Year 9 persuasive capabilities (reaserch.md — Year 9 marking criteria: substantiated viewpoints; purposeful selection and experimentation with structures, language features and voice; rhetorical strategies and evidence). Adapt to the specific theme/format — don't use verbatim if a more specific version fits.

**Provenance note (Q-001):** Year 10 phrasings are derived from the Year 9 elaborations.

## Viewpoint & argument
- I can state a clear, arguable viewpoint on a social theme.
- I can order my reasons so the argument escalates.
- I can develop each reason — why it holds and why my audience should act.
- I can make my evidence carry the argument, not decorate it.
- I can acknowledge the strongest counterargument and rebut it.

## Rhetoric & audience
- I can use a rhetorical device for a deliberate effect, not as decoration.
- I can balance logical, emotional and credibility appeals.
- I can sustain a voice that fits the format (feature article, letter to the editor, pitch, review).
- I can end with a call to action that follows from my argument.

## Vocabulary / register
- I can write in a formal public register (no slang, no "you guys").
- I can control modality deliberately (might → should → must).
- I can use rhetorical metalanguage to talk about my choices (e.g. "appeal", "rebuttal", "tricolon").

## Learning intention stems
- "We are learning to build a substantiated viewpoint on a social theme."
- "We are learning to deploy rhetoric deliberately, not decoratively."
- "We are learning to sustain a public voice across a whole text."
