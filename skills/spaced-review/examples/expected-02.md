# Expected behaviour for sample-02

The response must exhibit ALL of these (wording will vary):

**Cold start handled honestly.** The response acknowledges — plainly and warmly — that this is the student's first session, so the warm-up starts with the building blocks. It must NOT pretend to remember any history (no "last time we worked on…", no reference to past sessions, criteria, or coaching).

**2 generic items from the imaginative fundamentals** (the guide's cold-start menu), leaning toward what the task (a story opening about a noise in the dark) demands. The strongest candidates:
1. **Showing vs telling** — e.g. "In one sentence: what is the difference between showing and telling an emotion?" or apply-in-one-line: "Write one sentence that shows a character is scared without using the word *scared*."
2. **Complication / hook or point of view** — e.g. "What does a complication do in a story?" or "What point of view will your opening use — and in one line, why does it suit a noise-in-the-dark story?"

**Items are retrieval, not teaching.** Each item is answerable in a sentence or two; no rules explained, no worked examples, no answers given away inside the `## Warm-up` section.

**Self-check answers included** — one line per item under `## Check yourself`, matching the item count.

**Year 9–10 calibration.** Metalanguage appropriate to the band (showing/telling, complication, point of view, tone) is fine; no senior-only terminology dumped on a first session.

## Fail conditions

- Invented history: any reference to prior sessions, past scores, or previously coached patterns.
- More than 3 items, or items needing paragraph-length answers (second lesson).
- Teaching inside the warm-up (rules, explanations, worked examples).
- Answers revealed inside `## Warm-up`, missing from `## Check yourself`, or mismatched with the items.
- Cold clinical tone, or a fake-familiar tone that pretends a relationship exists.
