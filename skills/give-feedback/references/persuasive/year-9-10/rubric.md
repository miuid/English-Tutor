# QCAA A–E feedback rubric (Year 9–10 persuasive)

Based on QCAA five-point (A–E) standard elaborations for Year 9 (reaserch.md — Evaluative Standards & Marking Rubrics: Year 9 rows) read against the persuasive domain: viewpoints on social themes expressed through rhetorical strategies and evidence, in public formats — feature articles, letters to the editor, campaign pitches, formal reviews (reaserch.md — Persuasive formats, Year 9 row). The NAPLAN persuasive criteria (reaserch.md — The NAPLAN Writing Evaluation Scale) inform the criterion set below. Criterion-referenced: judge against the descriptor, not against other students.

**Provenance note (Q-001):** Year 9 descriptors below quote/paraphrase the Year 9 standard elaborations in reaserch.md. **Year 10 descriptors are derived** by extrapolating the same elaborations one band up — they are not verbatim QCAA text and must be treated as provisional until official sources are imported.

## The band shift: supported opinion → substantiated viewpoint

Year 8 asks students to advance an idea with supporting evidence. Year 9–10 asks them to *shape meaning and provide substantiation* — purposeful selection and considered experimentation with text structures, language features and features of voice (reaserch.md — Marking Criteria Year 9). Feedback at this band should push toward rhetorical control, not just more reasons.

## Levels (general shape)

- **A** — *purposeful creation* of a persuasive text: a discriminating viewpoint, substantiated; rhetoric deployed deliberately; voice sustained and format-appropriate throughout.
- **B** — effective viewpoint with strong substantiation; mostly purposeful rhetorical choices with minor lapses.
- **C** — sound: a clear viewpoint with developed reasons and relevant evidence; standard structures and some rhetorical strategies.
- **D** — partial: viewpoint general or inconsistent; assertion outweighs substantiation; devices formulaic.
- **E** — basic *statements* of opinion; fragmented structures; sporadic language features.

## Per-criterion descriptors

| Criterion | A/B | C | D/E |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Viewpoint & ideas** | Discriminating contention framed against the social theme; ideas expanded and substantiated | Clear viewpoint responding to the task with relevant reasons | Opinion undeveloped or both-sides summary |
| **Argument & substantiation** | Reasons ordered for escalation, fully elaborated, evidence load-bearing; counterargument rebutted where invited | Developed reasons with relevant evidence; no rebuttal | Assertion stacked on assertion; decorative evidence |
| **Rhetoric & audience** | Appeals (ethos/pathos/logos) balanced deliberately; devices varied and chosen for effect; register fits the public format | Some rhetorical strategies used; register mostly fits | No deliberate devices, or devices stacked without effect; register breaks |
| **Structure & cohesion** | Argument architecture: framing intro, escalating reasons, synthesising conclusion with final appeal | Functional intro/body/conclusion; paragraphs unified | No discernible architecture; conclusion repeats |
| **Language & voice** | Controlled, sustained voice; precise persuasive vocabulary and modality | Generally clear and mostly formal; some lapses | Informal register in a formal format; vague or repetitive wording |

## Mapping to the coaching skills

- Viewpoint / Argument weak → next step routes to `check-structure` (contention, substantiation, architecture).
- Rhetoric / Language weak → next step routes to `elevate-vocabulary` (appeals, modality, register).

## The Year 9–10 C→A lever

Most Year 9–10 persuasive writing sits at C because the argument is sound but the rhetoric is decorative. The highest-leverage next step is almost always: **make the rhetoric load-bearing — order the reasons to escalate, weave the evidence into the appeal, and rebut the strongest counterargument before the final call to action.**
