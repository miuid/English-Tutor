# QCAA A–E feedback rubric (Year 8 persuasive)

Based on QCAA five-point (A–E) standard elaborations for Year 8 (reaserch.md — Evaluative Standards & Marking Rubrics: Year 8 rows) read against the persuasive domain — "employs arguments, rhetoric, and evidence to sway an audience" (reaserch.md — Core Exam & Assessment Domains). The NAPLAN persuasive criteria (reaserch.md — The NAPLAN Writing Evaluation Scale: Audience, Text Structure, Ideas, Persuasive Devices, Vocabulary, Cohesion, Paragraphing) inform the criterion set below. Criterion-referenced: judge against the descriptor, not against other students.

## Levels (general shape)

- **A** — *purposeful creation* of a persuasive text that advances a clear position with supporting evidence; purposeful variation of structures, language features, and considered features of voice suited to the audience.
- **B** — effective persuasion; clear position; mostly purposeful choices with minor lapses in voice or development.
- **C** — sound: a position with relevant reasons and some supporting evidence; standard structures and language features.
- **D** — partial: position unclear or undeveloped; assertion dominates; voice doesn't fit the audience.
- **E** — basic *statements* of opinion; fragmented structures; sporadic language features.

## Per-criterion descriptors

| Criterion | A/B | C | D/E |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Position & ideas** (NAPLAN: Ideas) | Clear, arguable contention sustained; ideas selected and developed to sway this audience | A position responding to the task with relevant reasons | Opinion stated but undeveloped, or no position taken |
| **Argument & evidence** | Reasons elaborated — why each holds and why the audience should care; specific, relevant support | Reasons listed with some support; elaboration thin | Assertion only; no support, or support irrelevant |
| **Audience & voice** (NAPLAN: Audience, Persuasive Devices) | Voice and devices deliberately suit the stated audience (formal or informal); direct address and appeals used for effect | Generally suits the audience with lapses; a device or two present | Wrong register for the audience; no deliberate devices |
| **Structure & cohesion** (NAPLAN: Text Structure, Cohesion, Paragraphing) | Hook + contention + ordered reasons + call to action; one reason per paragraph; links reinforce the case | Functional intro/body/conclusion; paragraphs mostly unified | No discernible structure; conclusion repeats or is missing |
| **Language & vocabulary** (NAPLAN: Vocabulary) | Precise persuasive verbs; deliberate modality; intensifiers earned | Clear, mostly appropriate word choices | Vague, repetitive, or mismatched word choices |

## Mapping to the coaching skills

- Position / Argument weak → next step routes to `check-structure` (contention, E1 elaboration).
- Audience & voice / Language weak → next step routes to `elevate-vocabulary` (register + modality).

## The Year 8 C→A lever

Most Year 8 persuasive writing sits at C because reasons are listed rather than developed. The highest-leverage next step is almost always: **develop one reason into a full argument — why it holds, and why this audience should care — and land the call to action.**
