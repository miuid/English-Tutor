# QCAA A–E feedback rubric (Year 9–10 analytical)

Based on QCAA five-point (A–E) standard elaborations for Year 9 analytical writing (reaserch.md — Evaluative Standards & Marking Rubrics: Year 9 rows; Objectives for Analytical Essay Writing). Criterion-referenced: judge against the descriptor, not against other students.

**Provenance note (Q-001):** Year 9 descriptors below quote/paraphrase the Year 9 standard elaborations in reaserch.md. **Year 10 descriptors are derived** by extrapolating the same elaborations one band up — they are not verbatim QCAA text and must be treated as provisional until official sources are imported.

## The band shift: explanation → critical analysis

Year 8 asks students to *explain* how language features shape meaning. Year 9–10 asks them to *analyse and evaluate*: how representations of people, places, events and concepts respond to historical, social and cultural contexts, and how texts position readers (reaserch.md — Structural Framework; Marking Criteria Year 9). Feedback at this band should push toward that analytical move, not just more explanation.

## Levels (general shape)

- **A** — *considered analysis* of representations, contexts and positioning; *discriminating thesis* sustained; purposeful selection and considered experimentation with structures and language features.
- **B** — effective analysis; clear thesis; mostly purposeful choices with minor lapses.
- **C** — sound analysis of representations; a thesis responding to the prompt with relevant evidence; standard structures and language features.
- **D** — partial: explains rather than analyses; retelling or technique-spotting dominates; thesis unclear.
- **E** — basic *statements* about obvious representations and literal details; fragmented structures.

## Per-criterion descriptors

| Criterion | A/B | C | D/E |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Understanding of text / ideas** | Purposeful, substantiated interpretation of representations, cultural assumptions and values | Sound interpretation of representations and ideas | Statements about obvious/literal details only |
| **Analysis (how techniques create meaning)** | Considered analysis linking technique → effect → representation/context → reader positioning | Analyses how features create meaning, but stops short of context or positioning | Names techniques or quotes with no analysis |
| **Use of evidence** | Well-chosen, embedded, analysed; builds the argument | Relevant evidence supporting key points | Missing, irrelevant, or quote-dumped |
| **Structure & cohesion** | Discriminating thesis sustained; purposeful paragraphing and cohesion | Thesis responds to the prompt; functional paragraph organisation | No clear thesis; disorganised or retelling-driven |
| **Language & vocabulary** | Precise academic register; analytical metalanguage used with control | Generally clear, mostly formal; some lapses into informal register | Imprecise, repetitive, or conversational register |

## Mapping to the coaching skills

- Analysis / Evidence weak → next step routes to `check-structure` (E1 depth: effect → representation → positioning).
- Language & vocabulary weak → next step routes to `elevate-vocabulary` (register transition + band-appropriate metalanguage).

## The Year 9–10 C→A lever

Most Year 9–10 analytical writing sits at C because the student explains a technique's effect but never evaluates *why the writer made that choice* — how it constructs a representation or positions the reader. The highest-leverage next step is almost always: **extend the analysis from "what effect" to "how this positions the reader / what representation it builds".**
