# QCAA senior feedback rubric (Year 11–12 analytical — IA2 + EA)

Based on the official QCAA English 2025 v1.3 General Senior Syllabus (© State of Queensland (QCAA) 2026, CC-BY 4.0; `research/official/english-2025-v1.3-syllabus.md`): IA2 ISMG pp. 40–42, EA specifications p. 48, analytical-essay genre note p. 12. Criterion-referenced: judge against the descriptor, not against other students. ISMG→A–E mapping strategy per `research/qce-senior-instrument-modelling.md` (ISS-024).

## Criteria: the three official ISMG criterion names

Senior feedback uses the official criterion names so per-criterion scores line up 1:1 with the school's provisional marks (ISS-024 mapping strategy). IA2 mark ranges: **Knowledge application /9, Organisation and development /8, Textual features /8** (total /25, p. 40).

The qualifier ladder is shared across all three criteria and bridges to A–E: **discerning (≈A) → effective (≈B) → suitable/adequate (≈C) → superficial/inconsistent (≈D) → fragmented (≈E)** (ISMG pp. 40–42; reporting standards pp. 15–16).

## Levels (general shape)

- **A — discerning**: analysis of perspectives/representations *and* the cultural assumptions, attitudes, values and beliefs underpinning them *and* how audiences are invited to take up positions (KA 8–9/9); discerning genre control, synthesis and cohesion (OD 7–8/8); discerning language choices (TF 7–8/8).
- **B — effective**: the same three analysis moves, with less precision or consistency (KA 6–7/9).
- **C — suitable/adequate**: adequate analysis of representations and underpinning values; suitable genre control and organisation; mostly accurate language (KA 5/9, OD 4/8, TF 4/8).
- **D — superficial/inconsistent**: identifies representations and some values rather than analysing them; narrow subject-matter selection; inconsistent language choices (KA 3–4/9).
- **E — fragmented**: fragmented analysis, selection and language (KA 1–2/9).

## Per-criterion descriptors

| Criterion | A/B (discerning/effective) | C (suitable/adequate) | D/E (superficial/fragmented) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Knowledge application** (/9) | Discerning/effective analysis of perspectives and representations; of the cultural assumptions, attitudes, values and beliefs underpinning the texts and inviting audiences to take up positions; of the effects of aesthetic features and stylistic devices | Adequate analysis of the same three moves | Identifies representations/values/devices rather than analysing them |
| **Organisation and development** (/8) | Discerning/effective use of article/column/blog/essay conventions and the role of the writer for a public audience; discerning selection and synthesis of subject matter; cohesive devices that emphasise ideas | Suitable conventions; suitable selection, adequate synthesis; suitable organisation and cohesion | Narrow selection; disjointed or fragmented organisation |
| **Textual features** (/8) | Discerning/effective language choices, grammatically accurate/appropriate structures, mode-appropriate features for purpose | Suitable language choices; mostly accurate grammar | Inconsistent or fragmented language and grammar |

## EA (exam-mode analytical) note

The EA is the same analytical essay under examination conditions: an **unseen question** on a literary text from the EA prescribed-text-list section, for an audience with a deep understanding of the text, 15 min planning + 120 min working, no notes (p. 48). Coach toward the same three criteria; in exam mode also check the thesis answers the *unseen* question. The EA is developed and marked by the QCAA — the product never predicts an official EA mark.

## Provisional A–E boundary (never cross it)

Only the QCAA determines the official subject result after confirmation (p. 16). A–E bands shown to the student are **provisional coaching indicators** from the qualifier ladder — never present them as official results or predicted marks.

## Mapping to the coaching skills

- Knowledge application weak → next step routes to `check-structure` (E1 depth: representation → cultural assumptions/values → positioning).
- Organisation and development weak → next step routes to `check-structure` (essay-level: synthesis, genre control, cohesion).
- Textual features weak → next step routes to `elevate-vocabulary` (register + official metalanguage) or `fix-mechanics` (grammar accuracy) per the diagnosis.

## The senior C→A lever

Most senior analytical writing sits at C because the student analyses the representation but never names the **cultural assumptions/values** beneath it or the **position** the audience is invited to take up. The highest-leverage next step is almost always: **extend the analysis from representation to the values that underpin it and the audience positioning it invites** — that is what moves Knowledge application from "adequate" (5/9) toward "discerning" (8–9/9).
