# Expected behaviour for sample-01

The response must exhibit ALL of these (wording will vary):

**Reads the craft accurately.** The events are all present and in order (that's structure's lane — not the issue here); the key moment — hearing a noise in the dark — is told in one summarised sentence; the emotions are named with labels ("very scared", "terrified", "so relieved") rather than shown. Must NOT claim the story lacks a complication or praise the noise-in-the-dark moment as shown.

**Focuses on exactly ONE craft move.** The correct pick by the priority rule is **key moment told in summary** (type 1) — e.g. slow down the moment he hears the noise into real time, one concrete detail at a time. Picking named emotions (type 2) instead is acceptable only if the summarised key moment is explicitly named as "next time"; flagging both as the focus is a fail.

**Names what works specifically** — e.g. the story picks exactly the right moment to linger on (a noise in the dark is a genuinely scary beat), or the wallaby reveal is a nice tension-and-release. No empty praise.

**Models the repair on a DIFFERENT scene** (e.g. a first day at a new school, a wave about to break), showing a moment slowed down through the body or one sense in 1–3 sentences. Must NOT rewrite or complete the student's bush sentences.

**Hands the work back**: asks the student to rewrite only the noise-in-the-dark moment themselves, slowing it down; a sentence stem or frame (e.g. "The first thing I heard was… My hands…") is appropriate scaffolding at Year 8.

**Ends with a concrete "I can…" criterion**, e.g. "I can slow down my story's biggest moment and show it, not just tell it."

**Does NOT comment on plot structure or individual word choice** — events-listed structure is `check-structure`'s lane; flat verbs are `elevate-vocabulary`'s lane.

**Register**: encouraging Year 8 voice; treats the told moment and named feelings as a sound start, not a failure.

## Fail conditions
- Rewrites the student's bush scene or supplies the showing sentences for the noise moment (ghostwriting).
- Flags two or more craft issues as the focus (unbounded).
- Claims the noise-in-the-dark moment is already shown, or that the story has no key moment (misread).
- Coaches paragraph/scene ordering or verb upgrades — out of lane.
- Describes the named emotions or told moment as a failure rather than the C-standard starting point.
