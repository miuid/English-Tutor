# Expected behaviour for sample-02

The response must exhibit ALL of these (wording will vary):

**Reads the craft accurately.** The opening shows real moment-level craft inside the narrator's lens (palm on the wall over the height marks, counting the boxes "like I was counting down to something I didn't want to name" — shown, not told). The fault is the lens itself: mid-scene the narration enters Mum's head ("thinking about the first dinner she had ever cooked"), then names the narrator externally in third person ("Marcus wondered…"), with no signal — drift, not design. Must NOT criticise the wall/boxes beats as told-in-summary.

**Focuses on exactly ONE craft move.** The correct pick by the priority rule is **unsignalled POV shift** (type 3) — the brief asks for one consistent lens, and the drift confuses who is seeing. Either holding the "I" lens throughout, or converting the shift into deliberate design (scene break / marked section, each lens with its own voice), is the repair. Flagging any second issue as the focus is a fail.

**Names what works specifically** — e.g. the height-marks detail carries the goodbye without naming it, or "something I didn't want to name" is exactly what showing looks like. No empty praise.

**Models the repair on a DIFFERENT scene** (e.g. a last-match-before-the-final-whistle scene), showing either a held single lens or a signalled shift in 1–3 sentences. Must NOT rewrite the student's moving-away sentences.

**Hands the work back**: asks the student to rework the paragraph so the lens is either held or deliberately signalled, in their own words; a frame (e.g. a scene-break marker plus "In the kitchen, Mum…" as a clearly separate section) is appropriate scaffolding at Year 9.

**Ends with a concrete "I can…" criterion**, e.g. "I can hold one narrator lens — or signal the shift so my reader always knows whose eyes they're behind."

**Does NOT comment on word choice, imagery polish, or plot structure** — those belong to `elevate-vocabulary` and `check-structure`.

## Fail conditions
- Rewrites the student's paragraph or supplies the held-lens version of the moving-away scene (ghostwriting).
- Flags two or more craft issues as the focus (unbounded).
- Treats the drift as deliberate design, or flags a signalled shift as a fault (misread of the lens).
- Criticises the shown wall/boxes beats as telling (misread of the moment).
- Drifts into vocabulary or structure commentary — out of lane.
