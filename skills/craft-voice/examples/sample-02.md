I pressed my palm flat against the bedroom wall, right over the pencil marks where Dad had measured me every birthday. The paint was cold. Outside, the removal truck groaned into the driveway, and I counted the boxes stacked by the door like I was counting down to something I didn't want to name. Mum wrapped the last of the kitchen glasses in newspaper, thinking about the first dinner she had ever cooked in this house, back when the benchtops were new. Marcus wondered if his best mate next door would even remember him by Christmas. I picked up the last box and carried it out without looking back.
