# Voice craft (Year 9–10 imaginative)

Used by `craft-voice` to find and repair the one craft move that would most lift a narrative. Anchored to the QCAA imaginative domain — "conveys meaning and perspectives through narrative structure" (reaserch.md — Core Exam & Assessment Domains) — and the Year 9 expectation of manipulated narrative voice, controlled tone, and experimentation the reader can follow (reaserch.md — Imaginative formats, Year 9 row; Marking Criteria Year 9).

**Provenance note (Q-001):** Year 10 expectations are derived from the Year 9 elaborations, not verbatim QCAA text.

## The three craft dials

A narrative reaches the reader through three dials. Check each one:

**key moment (shown ↔ told) → emotion (shown ↔ named) → narrator (steady ↔ drifting)**

At this band a fourth dial joins: **tone (controlled ↔ whiplash)** — the register of the telling must hold or shift *on purpose*.

## Issue types, in priority order

| # | Issue type | What it looks like at Year 9–10 | The repair to coach |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Turning point told in summary** | The climactic beat of a 600–800 word piece is compressed into a sentence; the build-up gets more words than the payoff. | Slow the turning point: expand it into a scene with beats — let tension, not summary, carry the climax. |
| 2 | **Thin or clichéd imagery at the key beat** | The pivotal image is stock ("tears rolled down her cheeks", "my heart was pounding") or absent; the moment has no chosen, specific picture. | Choose ONE specific image that only fits *this* scene; let it carry the beat instead of a stock phrase. |
| 3 | **Unsignalled POV shift (drift, not design)** | The lens moves mid-scene with no signal — first person slips to third, or a second head is entered — and the reader must re-orient. At Year 9–10 a *signalled* shift (break, alternating chapters, marked sections) is a legitimate device (reaserch.md — Imaginative formats, Year 9 row: manipulated narrative voice); drift is the unsignalled kind. | Either hold one lens, or convert the shift into design: signal it (scene break, new section) and give each lens a distinct voice. |
| 4 | **Tone whiplash** | The register of the telling lurches — comic aside inside an elegy, slang inside a formal monologue — without a deliberate effect. Monologue and voice-driven formats (reaserch.md — Imaginative formats, Year 9 row) live or die on this dial. | Name the tone the piece is committed to; shift it only where the shift *means* something. |

## Priority rule

Choose the ONE focus issue as the **lowest-numbered issue type present**. A summarised turning point (1) outranks thin imagery (2), which outranks drift (3) and whiplash (4): the moment must open up before its picture or lens matters. Never flag a signalled POV shift or a purposeful tonal turn — at this band, deliberate control is the A/B discriminator, not a fault.

## Band calibration

- A controlled linear telling with named emotions is **sound C-range work** — flag the missing *deliberateness* (an opened-up turning point, a chosen image), not the absence of tricks (reaserch.md — Marking Criteria Year 9: considered experimentation is the A/B move, and "considered" means the reader can follow it).
- Clichéd imagery is minor anywhere *except* the key beat; a stock image at the climax is major (type 2).
- Ambition is not the problem at this band — control is. Coach the smallest move that makes the daring choice *readable*.

## Modelling rule

Model every repair on a **different scene** (e.g. if the student wrote a leaving-home story, model on a last-match-before-the-final-whistle scene), 1–3 sentences, then hand the student's own sentence back untouched.

## The Year 9–10 C→A lever

The single highest-leverage repair at this band: **open up the turning point into a real-time scene carried by one chosen image, inside one deliberate lens.** Voice that the reader can follow is what separates experimentation from accident.
