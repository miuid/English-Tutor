# Voice craft (Year 8 imaginative)

Used by `craft-voice` to find and repair the one craft move that would most lift a narrative. Anchored to the QCAA imaginative domain — "conveys meaning and perspectives through narrative structure" (reaserch.md — Core Exam & Assessment Domains) — and the Year 8 Writing/Creating criterion: purposeful creation, with A-standard work showing *considered features of voice that orient, engage, and affect the reader* (reaserch.md — Marking Criteria Year 8; A–E standard elaborations). Showing strategy also serves the NAPLAN narrative Audience and Character & Setting criteria (reaserch.md — The NAPLAN Writing Evaluation Scale).

## The three craft dials

A narrative reaches the reader through three dials. Check each one:

**key moment (shown ↔ told) → emotion (shown ↔ named) → narrator (steady ↔ drifting)**

## Issue types, in priority order

| # | Issue type | What it looks like at Year 8 | The repair to coach |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Key moment told in summary** | The scene the story exists for — the discovery, the fright, the goodbye — is compressed into one sentence ("then the monster appeared and I was terrified"). The events are right; the moment never slows down. | Slow the moment down: one scene, real time, one concrete detail at a time. Let the reader be *in* it. |
| 2 | **Emotions named, not shown** | Feeling labels do the work ("she was sad", "it was scary", "he was so relieved") with no physical or sensory evidence. This is the **expected C ceiling** at Year 8 (reaserch.md — A–E elaborations: C = standard language features, A/B = purposeful). | Give the body a job or give one sense a detail: hands, breath, feet, sound — show the evidence and let the reader name the feeling. |
| 3 | **POV drift** | The lens slips without a signal: "I" becomes "he", or the narration enters a second character's head mid-scene. **Major** only when it confuses who is seeing or speaking; a single slip is minor (reaserch.md — Marking Criteria Year 8: voice must orient the reader). | Pick one lens and hold it; if a shift is wanted, signal it (scene break, new chapter) so it reads as design, not drift. |

## Priority rule

Choose the ONE focus issue as the **lowest-numbered issue type present**. A summarised key moment (1) outranks scattered emotion labels (2): if the big scene never opens up, polishing smaller beats changes nothing. Never flag a *signalled* POV shift — deliberate design is the A-standard move, not a fault.

## Band calibration

- Named emotions and a told key moment are **sound, not failing** — name the moment and the feeling as what works, coach the missing showing. Do not describe a C-standard move as a mistake.
- At Year 8, "showing" means one scene slowed down with one or two concrete details — not a page of sensory overload (AERO explicit instruction: teach the move small enough to imitate, then fade; teacher-skills.md).
- Year 8 imaginative staples — short stories, narrative interventions, text transformations, memoirs, diaries (reaserch.md — Imaginative formats, Year 8 row) — live or die on the *key moment*; coach that first, always.

## Modelling rule

Model every repair on a **different scene** (e.g. if the student wrote about getting lost in the bush, model on a first day at a new school or a wave about to break), 1–3 sentences, then hand the student's own sentence back untouched.

## The Year 8 C→A lever

The single highest-leverage repair at this band: **slow down the key moment and show it — real time, one concrete detail through one sense, the feeling carried by the body instead of the label.** That is the difference between recounting events and crafting an experience.
