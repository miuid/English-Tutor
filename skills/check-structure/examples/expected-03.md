# Expected behaviour for sample-03

The response must exhibit ALL of these (wording will vary), calibrated to the **year-11-12 rubric** (IA2 + EA framework):

**Structure snapshot** roughly:
`P (present — "power does not last" is an arguable claim, but thematic rather than a critical perspective on the representation) · E1 analysis (present but at the senior floor — effect explained, no cultural-assumptions/values or positioning move) · E2 evidence (present from both texts, embedded, under-analysed) · L (weak or missing) · synthesis (ABSENT — the two texts are treated as sequential halves)`

**What's working**: names something real and specific — e.g. the student has an arguable claim spanning both texts, embeds quotes from each, and *does* explain effects (dark imagery → danger; ruined statue → fallen power). The Year 9–10 evaluation floor is within reach.

**The ONE focus gap = E1 depth (representation → cultural assumptions/values → audience positioning)**, per year-11-12 rubric priority #2: the response explains effects but never names the values underpinning the representation of power or the position the audience is invited to take up. Must NOT re-teach "explain the effect" (already banked) or "evaluate the representation" (the Year 9–10 move, nearly there) — the senior gap is the values/positioning move. Must NOT try to fix synthesis, Link and vocabulary at the same time.

**Modelled on a DIFFERENT example** — e.g. extends an analysis of an unrelated line (not Macbeth, not Ozymandias, not about power) from "what representation" to "whose values it rests on and where the audience is invited to stand". Must NOT rewrite the student's own sentences.

**Try this**: prompts the student to add 1–2 sentences to their own analysis naming the cultural assumption behind one representation (e.g. what view of kingship or ambition does the ruined statue / the hidden "black and deep desires" rest on, and where does that invite the audience to stand?) — in their own words. A sentence stem is acceptable, e.g. "By ___, Shelley exposes the cultural assumption that ___, inviting the audience to ___."

**Success looks like**: an "I can…" criterion, e.g. "I can analyse the cultural assumptions and values that underpin a representation and how it positions the audience."

**Register**: encouraging, senior appropriate, formal-academic modelling; official metalanguage (*representation*, *cultural assumptions*, *positioning*) used precisely.

## Fail conditions
- Re-teaches the Year 8/9–10 move (explain the effect / evaluate the representation) as the gap — the student is at or near that floor.
- Rewrites the student's paragraph into a finished version.
- Lists more than one structural gap as the focus (e.g. also fixing synthesis or the missing Link).
- Corrects "shows" / "makes you feel" as vocabulary (out of scope — that's `elevate-vocabulary`).
- Praise is generic ("good job!").
