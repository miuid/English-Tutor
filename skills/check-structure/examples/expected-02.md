# Expected behaviour for sample-02

The response must exhibit ALL of these (wording will vary), calibrated to the **year-9-10 rubric**:

**Structure snapshot** roughly:
`P (present, arguable claim) · E1 explanation (present but shallow — effect explained, no evaluation) · E2 evidence (present, embedded, under-analysed) · L (weak or missing)`

**What's working**: names something real and specific — e.g. the student has an arguable point, an embedded quote, and *does* explain an effect (dark imagery → danger) — that Year 8 move is already banked.

**The ONE focus gap = E1 depth (effect → evaluation)**, per year-9-10 rubric priority #2: effect explained but no representation/context/positioning move. Must NOT re-teach "explain the effect" (the student already does it) and must NOT try to fix Link and vocabulary at the same time.

**Modelled on a DIFFERENT example** — e.g. shows how to extend an analysis of an unrelated line (not Macbeth, not ambition) from "what effect" to "how this positions the audience / what it says about the idea". Must NOT rewrite the student's own "Stars, hide your fires" sentence for them.

**Try this**: prompts the student to add 1–2 sentences evaluating *why Shakespeare wants the audience to see ambition this way* (e.g. Macbeth hiding his "black and deep desires" positions us to see ambition as shameful and secretive) — in their own words. A sentence stem is acceptable at this band's lower end, e.g. "By ___, Shakespeare positions the audience to see ambition as ___."

**Success looks like**: an "I can…" criterion, e.g. "I can explain how the text positions the reader to think or feel."

**Register**: encouraging, Year 9 appropriate, formal-academic modelling.

## Fail conditions
- Re-teaches the Year 8 move ("explain the effect") as the gap — the student already explains effects.
- Rewrites the student's paragraph into a finished version.
- Lists more than one structural gap as the focus.
- Corrects "shows" / "really good" as vocabulary (out of scope — that's `elevate-vocabulary`).
- Praise is generic ("good job!").
