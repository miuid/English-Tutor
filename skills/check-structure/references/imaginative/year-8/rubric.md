# Narrative structure rubric (Year 8 imaginative)

Used by `check-structure` to score each element of an imaginative response. Anchored to the QCAA imaginative domain — "conveys meaning and perspectives through narrative structure" (reaserch.md — Core Exam & Assessment Domains) — and the Year 8 Writing/Creating criterion: purposeful creation of texts with purposeful variation of text structures and language features (reaserch.md — Marking Criteria Year 8). Approved formats at this band: short stories, narrative interventions, text transformations, memoirs, and diaries (reaserch.md — Imaginative formats, Year 8 row).

## Element checks

| Element | Present & strong (A/B) | Present but weak (C) | Missing (D/E) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Orientation** | Opening lines establish who/where/when and hook the reader immediately. | Setting and character arrive eventually; opening is generic ("One day…"). | No orientation; reader can't tell who or where. |
| **Complication** | One clear problem/conflict drives the story — real-world or fictional ethical dilemmas are legitimate ground (reaserch.md — Imaginative formats, Year 8 row). | A problem exists but is trivial, unclear, or resolved as soon as it appears. | No complication — a sequence of events, not a story. |
| **Rising tension** | Events build: each scene makes the stakes higher or the problem harder. | Events listed ("and then… and then…") at a flat emotional level. | Plot jumps straight from problem to ending. |
| **Climax / key moment** | The turning point is slowed down and shown — the moment the story exists for. | The key moment is rushed or told in a sentence. | No discernible turning point. |
| **Resolution** | The ending follows from what happened and lands with meaning (change, cost, or insight). | Ending is abrupt, convenient, or just stops ("then I woke up"). | No ending. |

## Response-level checks (Year 8, 400–600 word extended / 2–4 min spoken-multimodal)

- **Paragraphing**: a break when time, place, or speaker changes (NAPLAN narrative criteria — Paragraphing; reaserch.md — The NAPLAN Writing Evaluation Scale).
- **Character consistency**: characters act in line with what the reader knows of them — "expanding on character actions" is the Year 8 brief (reaserch.md — Imaginative formats, Year 8 row).
- **Point of view**: one consistent POV held across the piece.
- **Voice**: the narrator sounds like a deliberate choice, not a default.

## Priority order for choosing the ONE focus gap

1. Missing or trivial **complication** (a list of events can't be coached into a story).
2. **Flat tension** — complication present but never built (the #1 Year 8 imaginative ceiling).
3. **Rushed climax** — the key moment told, not shown.
4. **Unearned resolution**.
5. **Paragraphing** that blurs scenes.

## The Year 8 C→A lever

Most Year 8 narratives sit at C because events are listed at one flat level. The highest-leverage next step is almost always: **build the complication — slow the key moment down, raise the stakes, and make the ending cost something.**
