# Narrative structure rubric (Year 9–10 imaginative)

Used by `check-structure` to score each element of an imaginative response. Anchored to the QCAA imaginative domain — "conveys meaning and perspectives through narrative structure" (reaserch.md — Core Exam & Assessment Domains) — and the Year 9 expectation: structural experimentation, manipulating narrative voice, changing character points of view, and playing with tone (reaserch.md — Imaginative formats, Year 9 row).

**Provenance note (Q-001):** Year 10 expectations are derived from the Year 9 elaborations, not verbatim QCAA text.

## The band shift: controlled arc → deliberate experimentation

Year 8 asks students to control a clear narrative arc. Year 9–10 asks for *purposeful selection and considered experimentation* with text structures, language features, and features of voice (reaserch.md — Marking Criteria Year 9, Writing/Creating). Approved formats stretch to multi-text narratives, serialized vlogs, script transformations, monologues, and digital stories (reaserch.md — Imaginative formats, Year 9 row). A non-linear or multi-voice structure is only a strength when it is *deliberate and controlled* — experimentation without control reads as fragmentation.

## Element checks

| Element | Present & strong (A/B) | Present but weak (C) | Missing (D/E) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Orientation** | Establishes situation and a distinct narrative voice from the first lines; may withhold information deliberately. | Standard orientation; voice generic. | Reader disoriented without evidence of design. |
| **Complication** | A layered problem — internal and/or external — that sustains 600–800 words. | A single clear problem, adequately developed. | No driving problem. |
| **Structural control** | Structure is a *choice*: non-linear time, shifting POV, or multi-text form used to shape meaning, and the reader can follow it. | Conventional linear arc, competently handled. | Structure fragmentary or confusing without payoff. |
| **Key moment(s)** | Turning points are slowed down, shown, and placed where the structure makes them land hardest. | Key moments present but rushed or told. | No discernible turning point. |
| **Resolution** | Ending resonates — it re-frames what came before, not just stops it. | Ending follows logically but adds no meaning. | Abrupt or missing ending. |
| **Tone** | Tone is played deliberately — shifts are controlled and signalled (reaserch.md — Imaginative formats, Year 9 row). | Consistent but unremarkable tone. | Accidental tonal whiplash. |

## Response-level checks (Year 9–10, 600–800 word extended / 3–5 min spoken / 4–6 min multimodal)

- **POV control**: shifts in point of view are a deliberate structural device, clearly signalled — not drift (reaserch.md — Imaginative formats, Year 9 row).
- **Voice manipulation**: narrator voice is a crafted instrument; monologue/script formats demand a voice that carries alone.
- **Cohesion across experimentation**: even fragmented or multi-text forms need semantic threads the reader can hold (NAPLAN — Cohesion).

## Priority order for choosing the ONE focus gap

1. **Uncontrolled experimentation** — structural tricks the reader can't follow (fix control before adding ambition).
2. Missing or thin **complication** for the length.
3. **Rushed key moment** — telling where the structure earned a shown scene.
4. **Unsignalled POV/tone shifts** (drift, not design).
5. **Flat resolution** that stops rather than resonates.

## The Year 9–10 → A+ move

At Year 8 the lever is building tension into one complication. At Year 9–10 that is the floor: the A move is **deliberate structural control** — a voice, structure, or tonal shift the reader can *feel was chosen*, with key moments shown and an ending that re-frames the whole piece.
