# Argument structure rubric (Year 8 persuasive)

Used by `check-structure` to score each element of a persuasive response. Anchored to the QCAA persuasive domain — "employs arguments, rhetoric, and evidence to sway an audience" (reaserch.md — Core Exam & Assessment Domains) — and the Year 8 Writing/Creating criterion: expressing and advancing ideas with supporting evidence using text structures and language features (reaserch.md — Marking Criteria Year 8). Paragraph logic adapts PEEL (Queensland English Tutoring Blueprint — PEEL/TEEL) from textual analysis to argument.

## Element checks

| Element | Present & strong (A/B) | Present but weak (C) | Missing (D/E) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Contention** | A clear, arguable position stated up front and sustained across the whole response. | A position exists but is vague, wishy-washy, or drifts between paragraphs. | No position; the piece discusses "both sides" without ever taking one. |
| **P — Point / Reason** | Each paragraph opens with one distinct reason that supports the contention. | Reasons exist but overlap, or a paragraph opens with an example instead of a reason. | No reasons; jumps straight into anecdotes or assertions. |
| **E1 — Elaboration** | Explains *why* the reason holds and *why the audience should care* — the persuasive equivalent of analysis. | Restates the reason in other words without developing it. | Absent; evidence dropped in with no reasoning. |
| **E2 — Evidence / example** | Specific, relevant support: fact, statistic, example, or scenario the audience accepts. | Generic support ("everyone knows…", "studies show…" with nothing behind it). | No support at all — pure assertion. |
| **L — Link** | Closes by tying the reason back to the contention and the audience. | A weak closing echo of the point. | Paragraph stops after the example. |

## Response-level checks (Year 8, 400–600 word extended / 2–4 min spoken)

- **Introduction**: orients the audience and states the contention; a hook is expected in spoken/multimodal formats (reaserch.md — Persuasive formats, Year 8 row).
- **Paragraph unity**: one reason per paragraph, each opening with its own Point.
- **Audience adaptation**: language and features of voice suit the stated audience — formal or informal (reaserch.md — Year 8 persuasive: "adapt language and features of voice to suit formal or informal audiences").
- **Conclusion**: reinforces the contention and ends with a call to action or memorable final appeal, not a summary repeat.

## Priority order for choosing the ONE focus gap

1. Missing or unclear **contention** (nothing else can be judged without it).
2. **Shallow E1** — reason asserted but never developed into why-it-matters (the #1 Year 8 persuasive ceiling).
3. **Evidence without elaboration** (E2 present, E1 missing) — assertion-stacking outranks everything except a missing contention.
4. Missing **Link**.
5. **Audience mismatch** — voice doesn't fit the stated audience.

## The Year 8 C→A lever

Most Year 8 persuasive writing sits at C because the student lists reasons without developing them. The highest-leverage next step is almost always: **turn one asserted reason into a developed argument — explain why it holds and why this audience should care.**
