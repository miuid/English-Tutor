# Argument structure rubric (Year 9–10 persuasive)

Used by `check-structure` to score each element of a persuasive response. Anchored to the QCAA persuasive domain — "employs arguments, rhetoric, and evidence to sway an audience" (reaserch.md — Core Exam & Assessment Domains) — and the Year 9 expectation of viewpoints on social themes expressed through rhetorical strategies and evidence (reaserch.md — Persuasive formats, Year 9 row).

**Provenance note (Q-001):** Year 10 expectations are derived from the Year 9 elaborations, not verbatim QCAA text.

## The band shift: assertion → substantiated viewpoint

Year 8 asks students to advance an idea with supporting evidence. Year 9–10 asks them to *expand and substantiate* a viewpoint — purposeful selection and considered experimentation with structures, language features and features of voice (reaserch.md — Marking Criteria Year 9, Listening/Speaking/Creating and Writing/Creating). Rhetoric becomes a deliberate, named craft, not decoration.

## Element checks

| Element | Present & strong (A/B) | Present but weak (C) | Missing (D/E) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Contention** | A *discriminating* viewpoint — specific, arguable, framed against the social theme, sustained throughout. | A clear position that responds to the task but stays general. | No position, or a summary of both sides. |
| **P — Point / Reason** | Each paragraph opens with a distinct reason that builds the case in a deliberate order. | Reasons exist but overlap or are listed rather than ordered. | No reasons; assertion or anecdote only. |
| **E1 — Elaboration** | Develops the reason: why it holds, what it implies, why this audience should act or agree. | Explains the reason but stops before implication or audience impact. | Absent. |
| **E2 — Evidence / example** | Well-chosen, specific, and woven into the rhetoric — evidence *is* the persuasion, not an add-on. | Relevant evidence, loosely integrated or asserted ("studies show"). | No support. |
| **L — Link** | Ties the reason back to the contention and escalates the argument. | Weak echo of the point. | Paragraph stops after the example. |
| **Rhetorical control** | Devices (e.g. tricolon, rhetorical question, direct address) are chosen for effect and varied. | Devices present but formulaic or repeated. | No deliberate devices. |

## Response-level checks (Year 9–10, 600–800 word extended / 3–5 min spoken / 4–6 min multimodal)

- **Introduction**: frames the social theme and lands a discriminating contention.
- **Argument architecture**: reasons ordered for escalation; at Year 9–10 a **counterargument acknowledged and rebutted** is a hallmark of the A/B range.
- **Voice and register**: a consistent, controlled voice suited to format — feature article, letter to the editor, campaign pitch, formal review (reaserch.md — Persuasive formats, Year 9 row).
- **Conclusion**: synthesises the case and ends on a call to action that follows from the argument.

## Priority order for choosing the ONE focus gap

1. Missing or general **contention**.
2. **Unsubstantiated argument** — reasons asserted, never developed or evidenced (the #1 Year 9–10 persuasive ceiling).
3. **Formulaic rhetoric** — devices spotted-and-stacked rather than deployed for effect.
4. Missing **rebuttal** where the task invites opposition.
5. Missing **Link** / weak escalation order.

## The Year 9–10 → A+ move

At Year 8 the lever is developing one reason. At Year 9–10 that is the floor: the A move is **substantiation plus rhetorical control** — evidence woven into a deliberate argument architecture, with a counterargument rebutted and voice sustained to the final appeal.
