# PEEL/TEEL structure rubric (Year 9–10 analytical)

Used by `check-structure` to score each element. Anchored to QCAA Year 9 standard elaborations (reaserch.md — Evaluative Standards) and the analytical-essay objectives: discriminating thesis, logical body paragraphs, integrated evidence.

**Provenance note (Q-001):** Year 10 expectations are derived from the Year 9 elaborations, not verbatim QCAA text.

## Element checks

| Element | Present & strong (A/B) | Present but weak (C) | Missing (D/E) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **P — Point / Topic sentence** | Opens with an arguable claim that advances the essay's thesis, not just the task. | A claim exists but is descriptive, plot-summary, or only loosely tied to the thesis. | No controlling idea; jumps into a quote or retelling. |
| **E1 — Explanation / Analysis** | Unpacks *how/why* the writer's choice works — technique → effect → representation/context → reader positioning. | Explains the effect of the technique but stops there (the classic Year 9 ceiling). | Absent; evidence dropped in with no analysis. |
| **E2 — Evidence** | Precise, well-chosen quote, smoothly embedded, analysed, and tied back to the thesis. | Quote present but loosely integrated or under-analysed. | No textual evidence. |
| **L — Link** | Connects the point back to the thesis and signals its significance to the argument. | A weak closing echo of the point. | Paragraph stops after the quote. |

## Essay-level checks (Year 9–10, 600–800 word extended response)

- **Thesis**: a *discriminating* position — specific, arguable, sustained across the whole response (reaserch.md — Objectives for Analytical Essay Writing).
- **Paragraph unity**: one idea per paragraph, each opening with its own Point that advances the thesis.
- **Cohesion**: logical progression and linking between paragraphs; the argument builds, not just lists.
- **Framing**: intro establishes the thesis and direction; conclusion synthesises the argument rather than repeating it.
- **Register**: formal academic voice throughout (see `elevate-vocabulary` tiers for the register transition).

## Priority order for choosing the ONE focus gap

1. Missing or unclear **Point/thesis** (nothing else can be judged without it).
2. **Shallow E1** — effect explained but no representation/context/positioning move (the #1 Year 9–10 analytical ceiling).
3. **Evidence without Explanation** (E2 present, E1 missing) — quote-dumping still outranks everything except a missing point.
4. Missing **Link**.
5. **Wrong order** of otherwise-present elements.

## The Year 9–10 → A+ move

At Year 8 the C→A lever is explaining the technique's effect. At Year 9–10 that is the *floor*, not the ceiling: the A move is extending the explanation into **evaluation** — why the writer made the choice, what representation it constructs, and how it positions the reader. Coaching toward "so what does this make the audience think/feel about the idea, and why would the writer want that?" is the highest-leverage structural intervention.
