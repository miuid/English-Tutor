# PEEL/TEEL structure rubric (Year 11–12 analytical — IA2 + EA)

Anchored to the official QCAA English 2025 v1.3 General Senior Syllabus (January 2026, © State of Queensland (QCAA) 2026, CC-BY 4.0; archived at `research/official/english-2025-v1.3-syllabus.md`): the analytical-essay genre note (p. 12), the IA2 instrument specifications and ISMG (pp. 38–42), and the EA examination specifications (p. 48). Framework mapping per `research/qce-senior-instrument-modelling.md` (ISS-024).

**The two official analytical instruments:**
- **IA2 — Written response for a public audience** (internal, 25%): analyse a representation of a concept, identity, time or place in **two connected texts** (at least one from the prescribed text list), with a focused perspective, in a genre suitable for print/online publication (article, blog, essay or column), up to **1500 words** (p. 38–39).
- **EA — Examination: extended response (analytical essay)** (external, 25%): exam-mode analytical essay on an **unseen question** about a literary text from the EA section of the prescribed text list, for an audience with a deep understanding of the text; 15 min planning + 120 min working, no notes (p. 48). Coach EA mode as the same structure under exam discipline.

## The senior genre contract (syllabus p. 12)

An analytical essay at senior level: informs the reader of an **interpretation** of a literary text; is structured around a **thesis** (the central argument); is substantiated by relevant **textual evidence** (the majority of evidence refers to the text); is written in a **formal tone** with **literary terminology** and academic conventions, for an **educated reader** familiar with the text. IA2 loosens the genre (article/column/blog) but not the analytical demand — the writer's role and relationship with a **public audience** must be established and maintained (IA2 objective 2, p. 38).

## Element checks (senior calibration)

| Element | Present & strong (discerning/effective) | Present but weak (suitable) | Missing (superficial/fragmented) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **P — Point / Topic sentence** | Opens with an arguable claim that advances a *critical perspective* on a representation, not just the topic. | A claim exists but describes the representation rather than interpreting it. | No controlling idea; jumps into retelling or a quote. |
| **E1 — Explanation / Analysis** | Unpacks how **aesthetic features and stylistic devices** construct the representation AND how **cultural assumptions, attitudes, values and beliefs** underpin the text and **invite audiences to take up positions** (ISMG Knowledge application, p. 40). | Analyses the technique's effect and the representation, but stops short of the cultural assumptions/values or positioning move. | Technique-spotting or effect-only explanation (the Year 9–10 ceiling — below the senior floor). |
| **E2 — Evidence** | Precise textual references, **selected and synthesised** to support the perspective (IA2 objective 6); in IA2, evidence connects **both** texts, not one after the other. | Relevant quotes from one text only, or two texts treated as sequential halves. | No textual evidence. |
| **L — Link** | Connects the point back to the thesis/perspective and its significance for the public conversation (IA2) or the unseen question (EA). | A weak closing echo. | Paragraph stops after the quote. |

## Essay-level checks

- **Thesis / perspective**: an *informed and critical perspective* (EA specifications, p. 48), sustained across the whole response; in IA2, a focused perspective with suitable scope (personal, philosophical, social, political or cultural) on the representation in both texts (p. 39).
- **Genre control (IA2)**: patterns and conventions of the chosen public-audience genre (article/column/blog/essay) used purposefully; the role of the writer is established and maintained (ISMG Organisation and development, p. 41).
- **Cohesion**: organisation and sequencing with cohesive devices that *emphasise ideas*, not just connect sentences (ISMG Organisation and development, p. 41).
- **Synthesis (IA2)**: the two texts are connected by the representation — integrated comparison, not two mini-essays.
- **Exam discipline (EA)**: thesis answers the *unseen* question directly; argument must stand without notes or the text.

## Priority order for choosing the ONE focus gap

1. Missing or unclear **thesis / critical perspective** (nothing else can be judged without it).
2. **Shallow E1** — representation analysed but no cultural-assumptions/values or positioning move (the #1 senior ceiling: caps Knowledge application at "adequate", 5/9).
3. **No synthesis across the two texts** (IA2) — sequential halves cap Organisation and development.
4. **Evidence without analysis** (E2 present, E1 missing).
5. **Genre/role-of-writer drift** (IA2) or missing **Link**.

## The senior A (discerning) move

At Year 9–10 the A move is evaluation (why the writer made the choice; what representation it constructs). At senior level that is the *floor*. **Discerning** analysis (8–9/9 Knowledge application) traces the representation to the **cultural assumptions, attitudes, values and beliefs** that underpin it and shows how the text **invites the audience to take up a position** — sustained as a critical perspective across the whole response. Coaching toward "whose values does this representation serve, and where does the text want its audience to stand?" is the highest-leverage structural intervention.
