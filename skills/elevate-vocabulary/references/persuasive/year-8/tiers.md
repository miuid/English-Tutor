# Vocabulary tiers reference (Year 8 persuasive)

Used by `elevate-vocabulary`. Based on the Tier 1/2/3 model (AERO explicit vocabulary instruction; teacher-skills.md — AERO Writing Instruction Model, Stage 1) and the Year 8 persuasive expectation of adapting language and features of voice to formal or informal audiences (reaserch.md — Persuasive formats, Year 8 row).

- **Tier 1** — everyday words (*good, bad, said, a lot*). Upgrade from these.
- **Tier 2** — academic/persuasive words used across subjects: *benefit, harmful, unfair, essential, responsible, community, consequence, impact*.
- **Tier 3** — persuasive metalanguage, always introduced with meaning on first use: *contention, audience, modality, direct address, rhetorical question, repetition, emotive language, call to action*.

## Flat word → stronger options (with shade of meaning)

| Flat word | Persuasive upgrades | Note on shade |
|---|---|---|
| **good** | beneficial, valuable, worthwhile, essential | "essential" = we cannot do without it — high modality. |
| **bad** | harmful, damaging, unfair, unacceptable | "unacceptable" = a judgement call to the audience's values. |
| **should** | must, need to, ought to | "must" is high modality — use when the argument can carry it. |
| **a lot / very** | significantly, overwhelmingly, constantly | intensity should live in the word, not "very". |
| **people think** | the community agrees, experts warn, evidence shows | name the source of the claim, not a vague crowd. |
| **I think / I believe** | (often delete — state it as fact) clearly, undoubtedly, the truth is | deleting "I think" raises modality; keep it only for personal appeal. |
| **thing** | issue, problem, consequence, choice, action | name what the thing *does* in the argument. |

## Modality ladder (the core persuasive dial)

Modality = how certain/forceful a statement sounds. Coach students to choose deliberately, not default to the middle.

| Low | Medium | High |
|---|---|---|
| might, could, perhaps | should, often, probably | must, always, never, clearly |

- High modality suits calls to action and rebuttals; overuse everywhere sounds ranty.
- Low modality has one good use at this band: conceding a point before turning it ("Admittedly, the change *may* cost more — but the alternative costs far more.").

## Audience register (Year 8 focus: adapt voice to the audience)

| Too casual for a formal audience | Formal-audience version |
|---|---|
| "You guys have to stop doing this" | "We must reconsider this practice" |
| "It's a really big problem" | "This is a significant and growing problem" |
| "Kids these days just don't care" | "Young people are disengaging from the issue" |

## Rules for suggesting

1. Meaning first: never offer a word whose connotation shifts the student's position.
2. Fit the audience: formal or informal voice must match the stated audience (reaserch.md — Year 8 persuasive).
3. Explain any Tier 3 word the first time it's introduced.
4. Prefer upgrading a persuasive verb or a modality choice over polishing a minor adjective.
5. Suggest devices by *effect*, not name-dropping: "ask your audience a question they can't ignore" before "use a rhetorical question".
