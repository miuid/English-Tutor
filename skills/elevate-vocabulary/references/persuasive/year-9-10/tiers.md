# Vocabulary tiers reference (Year 9–10 persuasive)

Used by `elevate-vocabulary`. Based on the Tier 1/2/3 model (AERO explicit vocabulary instruction; teacher-skills.md — AERO Writing Instruction Model, Stage 1) and the Year 9 persuasive expectation of rhetorical strategies and evidence in public formats — feature articles, letters to the editor, campaign pitches, formal reviews (reaserch.md — Persuasive formats, Year 9 row).

**Provenance note (Q-001):** the Year 10 ceiling is derived from the Year 9 elaborations, not verbatim QCAA text.

- **Tier 1** — everyday words (*good, bad, said*). Upgrade from these.
- **Tier 2** — at this band the floor rises: *benefit, harmful, significant* are baseline; growth moves to *imperative, inevitable, untenable, compelling, undermine, exacerbate, advocate*.
- **Tier 3** — rhetorical metalanguage. Year 8 set (*contention, audience, modality, rhetorical question, repetition, direct address, call to action*) is assumed; introduce the rhetoric set below, always with meaning on first use.

## Rhetorical metalanguage set (Tier 3, Year 9–10)

ethos (credibility appeal) · pathos (emotional appeal) · logos (logic/evidence appeal) · tricolon / rule of three · anaphora (repeated openings) · hyperbole · imperative · inclusive language ("we", "our") · connotation · juxtaposition · rebuttal / counterargument

## Flat word → stronger options (with shade of meaning)

| Flat word | Rhetorical / academic upgrades | Note on shade |
|---|---|---|
| **good** | beneficial, imperative, indispensable, commendable | "imperative" doubles as a noun for "an urgent necessity" — useful in calls to action. |
| **bad** | detrimental, untenable, reprehensible, counterproductive | "untenable" = cannot be defended — strong in rebuttals. |
| **very + adj** | (drop "very", strengthen the adjective) very important → critical, urgent | intensity lives in the word. |
| **shows** | reveals, exposes, underscores, demonstrates | "exposes" carries a critical edge — use when unveiling a problem. |
| **people** | the community, the public, stakeholders, future generations | name the group whose interest the argument invokes. |
| **we need to** | we must, it is imperative that, the time has come to | escalate toward the call to action, not before it. |

## Appeals in balance (the Year 9–10 lever)

Strong Year 9–10 persuasion balances the three appeals deliberately:

- **Logos** carries the argument (evidence, reasoning) — never let pathos substitute for it.
- **Pathos** opens and closes (hook and final appeal) — overuse mid-argument reads as manipulation.
- **Ethos** is built by naming credible sources and conceding fairly before rebutting.

## Register transition (Year 8 → Year 9–10)

| Year 8 floor | Year 9–10 target |
|---|---|
| "We should stop wasting so much food" | "Food waste is an untenable burden on both household budgets and the environment" |
| "Lots of people agree this is bad" | "Public consensus increasingly recognises the cost of inaction" |
| "Think about how you would feel" | "Consider the community we leave to the next generation" |

## Rules for suggesting

1. Meaning first: never offer a word whose connotation shifts the student's position.
2. Fit the format: a letter to the editor and a campaign pitch tolerate different registers — match the stated format (reaserch.md — Year 9 persuasive formats).
3. Explain any Tier 3 word the first time it's introduced.
4. Prefer upgrading modality, an appeal, or a register break over single-word polish.
5. Name the device only after the student has produced the effect — label as consolidation, not as a checklist to stuff in.
