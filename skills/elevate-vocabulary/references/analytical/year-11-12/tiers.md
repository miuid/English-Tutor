# Vocabulary tiers reference (Year 11–12 analytical — IA2 + EA)

Used by `elevate-vocabulary`. Based on the Tier 1/2/3 model (AERO explicit vocabulary instruction) and the official QCAA English 2025 v1.3 syllabus (© State of Queensland (QCAA) 2026, CC-BY 4.0; `research/official/english-2025-v1.3-syllabus.md`): the analytical-essay genre note requires a formal tone with relevant literary terminology for an educated audience (p. 12); the IA2/EA objectives name the senior analytical lexis — *perspectives, representations, cultural assumptions/attitudes/values/beliefs, aesthetic features, stylistic devices, positioning* (IA2 objectives 3–5, p. 38; EA objectives 3–5, p. 48). EA is exam-mode analytical: same lexis under exam conditions.

- **Tier 1** — everyday words (*good, said, big*). Upgrade from these.
- **Tier 2** — academic words used across subjects. At senior level the Year 9–10 growth set (*critique, substantiate, underscore, epitomise*) is now **baseline**; growth moves to *interrogate, problematise, subvert, underpin, foreground, juxtapose, privilege, marginalise*.
- **Tier 3** — analytical metalanguage. The Year 8/9–10 sets are assumed; the senior set below is the official syllabus vocabulary — introduce with meaning on first use.

## Flat word → stronger options (with shade of meaning)

| Flat word | Senior analytical upgrades | Note on shade |
|---|---|---|
| **shows** | constructs, represents, foregrounds, privileges, interrogates | "privileges" = gives one reading/value priority over others — a positioning word. |
| **says** (of a text) | contends, critiques, problematises, subverts, reinforces | "subverts" = undermines an expectation; "reinforces" = consolidates it. |
| **good / great** | discerning, effective, compelling, sophisticated | "discerning" is the top ISMG qualifier — reserve it for genuinely discriminating work. |
| **bad** | flawed, problematic, reductive, superficial | "superficial" is an official qualifier (mid-low ISMG band) — precise, not just negative. |
| **thing / stuff** | representation, perspective, aesthetic feature, stylistic device, cultural assumption | use the official terms — they are the words the ISMG descriptors are written in. |
| **makes you feel** | positions the audience to, invites the reader to take up, elicits | "invites audiences to take up positions" is the syllabus phrasing (IA2 objective 4). |
| **uses** (a technique) | deploys, manipulates, draws on, synthesises | "synthesises" fits IA2 objective 6 (select and synthesise subject matter). |
| **about society** | underpinned by cultural assumptions / attitudes / values / beliefs | name *which* assumption or value — the official criterion rewards the specificity. |
| **kind of / sort of** | arguably, to an extent — or delete | hedging must be analytical, never conversational. |
| **really / a lot** | significantly, profoundly, insistently | quantify the significance analytically. |

## Register transition (Year 9–10 → senior)

The senior shift is audience and precision: from a formal school essay to a piece for an **educated or public audience** that assumes familiarity with the text.

| Competent formal (Year 9–10 target) | Senior register (Year 11–12 target) |
|---|---|
| "Shakespeare uses dark imagery to show ambition is dangerous" | "Shakespeare's motif of darkness constructs ambition as a corrosive, self-concealing force" |
| "The writer makes the reader sympathise with her" | "The focalisation positions the reader to take up a sympathetic stance toward her" |
| "This text is about how society judges people" | "The representation is underpinned by cultural assumptions about respectability that the text invites its audience to interrogate" |
| "Both texts show power" | "Read against each other, the two texts synthesise a critique of power as performance" |

## Analytical metalanguage set (Tier 3, senior)

perspective · representation · cultural assumptions / attitudes / values / beliefs · positioning / invites the audience to take up a position · aesthetic features · stylistic devices · synthesis · genre conventions · role of the writer · public audience · informed and critical perspective · discerning / effective / suitable / superficial / fragmented (the official qualifier ladder)

## Rules for suggesting

1. Meaning first: never offer a word whose connotation shifts the student's point.
2. Fit the register and audience: formal, educated/public — clarity still beats ornament.
3. Explain any Tier 3 word the first time it's introduced.
4. Prefer the official syllabus term over a synonym when one exists — the ISMG descriptors reward that exact lexis.
5. Prefer replacing a repeated analytical verb or a register break over polishing a minor adjective.
