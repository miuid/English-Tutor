# Vocabulary tiers reference (Year 9–10 analytical)

Used by `elevate-vocabulary`. Based on the Tier 1/2/3 model (AERO explicit vocabulary instruction) and the Year 9 expectation of a formal academic register with precise analytical vocabulary (reaserch.md — Objectives: Technical Writing Mechanics, Grade A vs C).

**Provenance note (Q-001):** the Year 10 ceiling is derived from the Year 9 elaborations, not verbatim QCAA text.

- **Tier 1** — everyday words (*good, said, big*). Upgrade from these.
- **Tier 2** — academic words used across subjects. At Year 9–10 the expected floor rises: *convey, emphasise, significant* are now baseline; growth moves to *critique, substantiate, underscore, epitomise, convey nuance*.
- **Tier 3** — analytical metalanguage. Year 8 set (*imagery, metaphor, connotation, tone, positions the reader*) is assumed; introduce the representation/evaluation set below, always with meaning on first use.

## Flat word → stronger options (with shade of meaning)

| Flat word | Analytical / academic upgrades | Note on shade |
|---|---|---|
| **shows** | conveys, reveals, suggests, illustrates, demonstrates, highlights, foregrounds | "foregrounds" = brings to attention deliberately — a writer's-choice word. |
| **says** (of a text) | states, asserts, argues, implies, contends, critiques | "critiques" = evaluates negatively; don't use for neutral points. |
| **good / great** | effective, compelling, powerful, skilful, masterful | name *why* it's effective, not just that it is. |
| **bad** | ineffective, weak, unconvincing, flawed, problematic | "problematic" = raises issues — analytical, not just negative. |
| **thing / stuff** | element, feature, aspect, device, technique, representation | "representation" forces precision about *what is being constructed*. |
| **makes you feel** | evokes, elicits, positions the reader to feel, invites the audience to | "positions/invites" names the reader-effect deliberately. |
| **uses** (a technique) | employs, deploys, draws on, utilises, experiments with | "experiments with" fits Year 9 elaborations (purposeful experimentation). |
| **very + adj** | (drop "very", use a stronger adjective) very dark → ominous, foreboding | intensity should live in the word, not "very". |
| **kind of / sort of** | somewhat, arguably, to an extent — or delete | hedging has a place, but conversational hedges break register. |
| **really / a lot** | significantly, considerably, profoundly | quantify the significance analytically. |

## Register transition (Year 8 → Year 9–10)

The biggest band shift is register: from clear-but-conversational to formal academic voice.

| Conversational (Year 8 floor) | Academic register (Year 9–10 target) |
|---|---|
| "The writer makes you feel sorry for him" | "The writer positions the reader to sympathise with him" |
| "This quote is about how Macbeth wants to be king" | "This soliloquy reveals Macbeth's burgeoning ambition" |
| "Shakespeare uses lots of dark words" | "Shakespeare sustains a motif of darkness" |
| "It's kind of like he can't stop himself" | "Macbeth appears compelled by his own ambition" |

## Analytical metalanguage set (Tier 3, Year 9–10)

representation · positioning / positions the reader · context (historical, social, cultural) · perspective · aesthetic qualities · motif · juxtaposition · foreshadowing · dramatic irony · soliloquy · symbolism · subtext · substantiate · evaluate

## Rules for suggesting

1. Meaning first: never offer a word whose connotation shifts the student's point.
2. Fit the register: formal academic, but not overwrought — clarity beats ornament.
3. Explain any Tier 3 word the first time it's introduced.
4. Prefer replacing a repeated analytical verb ("shows") or a register break over polishing a minor adjective.
5. At this band, register upgrades ("you" → "the reader") rank as high as single-word swaps.
