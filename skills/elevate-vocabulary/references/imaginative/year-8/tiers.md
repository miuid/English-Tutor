# Vocabulary tiers reference (Year 8 imaginative)

Used by `elevate-vocabulary`. Based on the Tier 1/2/3 model (AERO explicit vocabulary instruction; teacher-skills.md — AERO Writing Instruction Model, Stage 1) and the Year 8 imaginative brief: creative plot development and expanding on character actions (reaserch.md — Imaginative formats, Year 8 row). Narrative vocabulary is judged on precision and range (NAPLAN — Vocabulary).

- **Tier 1** — everyday words (*said, walked, nice, scared, a lot*). Upgrade from these.
- **Tier 2** — precise narrative words used across writing: *trudged, hesitated, glanced, murmured, stifling, eerie, relentless, vast* — verbs and adjectives that carry the picture themselves.
- **Tier 3** — narrative metalanguage, always introduced with meaning on first use: *imagery, sensory detail, metaphor, simile, personification, foreshadowing, point of view, narrative voice, tension*.

## Flat word → stronger options (with shade of meaning)

| Flat word | Narrative upgrades | Note on shade |
|---|---|---|
| **said** | murmured, snapped, admitted, whispered | Each dialogue verb carries the emotion — but "said" is invisible and fine when the dialogue itself does the work. |
| **walked / went** | trudged, strode, crept, drifted | The verb shows *how* — trudged = tired or unwilling; crept = fearful or sneaky. |
| **scared** | frozen, heart hammering, couldn't breathe | Physical detail beats the label — show the body, not the word (see Show-don't-tell below). |
| **happy** | grinning, light on her feet, couldn't stop smiling | Same rule: the reader should *see* it. |
| **nice / good** | golden, gentle, effortless, warm | Pick the adjective that fits the exact thing, not a general approval. |
| **big** | vast, towering, swollen, endless | Scale + texture: "swollen" suits a river, "towering" a building. |
| **a lot / very** | (delete and strengthen the base word) "exhausted" not "very tired" | Intensity should live in the word, not "very". |

## Show-don't-tell upgrades (the core imaginative dial)

| Telling | Showing move |
|---|---|
| "She was nervous." | Give the body a job: "She rearranged the pens on her desk for the third time." |
| "The house was creepy." | One concrete detail through one sense: "The hallway light buzzed and died." |
| "He was angry." | Action or clipped dialogue: "He hung up mid-sentence." |

Rule of thumb: name the emotion only when the scene has already earned it — otherwise show the detail and let the reader name it.

## Rules for suggesting

1. Precision over ornament: a metaphor must earn its place; never decorate a sentence that needs a stronger verb.
2. One sense at a time: suggest a single concrete detail, not a sensory overload.
3. Explain any Tier 3 word the first time it's introduced.
4. Prefer upgrading the **key emotional beat** of the scene over polishing minor description.
5. Suggest devices by *effect*, not name-dropping: "let us hear the floorboards complain" before "use personification".
