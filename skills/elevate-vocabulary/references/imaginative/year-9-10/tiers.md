# Vocabulary tiers reference (Year 9–10 imaginative)

Used by `elevate-vocabulary`. Based on the Tier 1/2/3 model (AERO explicit vocabulary instruction; teacher-skills.md — AERO Writing Instruction Model, Stage 1) and the Year 9 imaginative brief: manipulating narrative voice, changing character points of view, and playing with tone (reaserch.md — Imaginative formats, Year 9 row).

**Provenance note (Q-001):** Year 10 expectations are derived from the Year 9 elaborations, not verbatim QCAA text.

- **Tier 1** — everyday words (*said, walked, nice, scared*). Upgrade from these.
- **Tier 2** — precise narrative words, now including tonal control: *wry, wistful, caustic, tender, defiant, measured* — words that set tone, not just picture.
- **Tier 3** — narrative metalanguage, always introduced with meaning on first use: *imagery, motif, symbolism, foreshadowing, irony, narrative voice, focalisation, tone, register shift*.

## The band shift: precision → tonal control

At Year 8 the job is precise, concrete language. At Year 9–10 the A/B discriminator is **voice and tone carried by word choice** (reaserch.md — Marking Criteria Year 9: considered experimentation with language features and features of voice). The same event narrated in a wry voice and a wistful voice should produce *different sentences*.

## Flat word → stronger options (with shade of meaning)

| Flat word | Narrative upgrades | Note on shade |
|---|---|---|
| **said** | deadpanned, conceded, offered, pressed | Dialogue verbs now calibrate voice: "deadpanned" signals irony; "conceded" signals subtext. |
| **looked** | studied, avoided, searched, measured | The verb reveals the relationship, not just the action. |
| **sad** | hollowed out, quieter than usual, went through the motions | Show the *pattern* of grief, not the label. |
| **angry** | cold, clipped, careful | Understatement often lands harder than heat at this band. |
| **beautiful** | light caught the edges, the kind of quiet you don't interrupt | Concrete image over abstract praise — the Year 9 move is the specific, not the grand. |

## Figurative control

| Device | Year 8 use | Year 9–10 control |
|---|---|---|
| **Metaphor / simile** | One apt comparison per scene. | Sustained or returned-to imagery that builds a **motif** across the piece. |
| **Symbolism** | Not expected. | An object or detail that accrues meaning — introduced plainly, weighted by the ending. |
| **Foreshadowing** | A hint the reader notices later. | Planted and paid off deliberately; the re-read should reward it. |
| **Irony / tone shifts** | Not expected. | Signalled, controlled shifts — the reader feels the turn, not the whiplash (reaserch.md — Imaginative formats, Year 9 row). |

## Rules for suggesting

1. Voice first: any word suggestion must fit the narrator's established voice — a precise word that breaks the voice is a downgrade.
2. Motif over ornament: prefer strengthening an image the piece already uses over adding a new one.
3. Explain any Tier 3 word the first time it's introduced.
4. Prefer upgrading the **key emotional beat or tonal turn** over polishing background description.
5. Suggest devices by *effect*, not name-dropping: "let that detail from the opening come back at the end with new weight" before "use a motif".
