# Argument chains (Year 8 persuasive)

Used by `strengthen-argument` to trace and repair the reasoning chain of a persuasive response. Anchored to the QCAA persuasive domain — "employs arguments, rhetoric, and evidence to sway an audience" (reaserch.md — Core Exam & Assessment Domains) — and the Year 8 Writing/Creating criterion: expressing and advancing ideas *with supporting evidence* (reaserch.md — Marking Criteria Year 8). Chain logic adapts PEEL from textual analysis to argument (Queensland English Tutoring Blueprint — PEEL/TEEL).

## The chain

A persuasive argument is a chain, and it is only as strong as its weakest link:

**contention → reason → elaboration (why it holds / why this audience should care) → evidence → link**

## Break types, in priority order

| # | Break type | What it looks like at Year 8 | The repair to coach |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Contention without reasons** | A position is stated, then the piece repeats it in different words or drifts into description; no distinct reason ever appears. | Generate ONE distinct reason that supports the position (idea generation, not wording). |
| 2 | **Reason without elaboration** | "School uniforms are unfair." Full stop — the *why* never comes. This is the **expected C ceiling** at Year 8 (reaserch.md — A–E elaborations: C = standard elaboration, A/B = purposeful). | Develop the reason: why does it hold, and why should *this* audience care? One "because… which means…" move. |
| 3 | **Decorative evidence** | "Everyone knows uniforms are uncomfortable." / "Studies show homework is bad." Support is asserted, not given; or an anecdote is stacked with no reasoning connecting it to the contention. | Anchor or explain the support: name the example/specific scenario, then spell out how it proves the reason. |
| 4 | **Missing rebuttal** | Only when the task invites opposition (e.g. "discuss both sides", a debate brief, a letter responding to critics). Year 8 speeches to a friendly audience do **not** require it. | Acknowledge the strongest opposing point in one line, then answer it ("Some say… but…"). |

## Priority rule

Choose the ONE focus break as the **lowest-numbered break type present**. An asserted-but-undeveloped reason (2) outranks decorative evidence (3): reasoning before decoration. Never coach a rebuttal a Year 8 task didn't ask for.

## Band calibration

- Asserted reasons are **sound, not failing** — name the reason as what works, coach the missing development. Do not describe a C-standard move as a mistake.
- At Year 8, elaboration means one clear *why-it-holds* plus *why-the-audience-should-care* — not a research paragraph. Keep the model small enough to imitate (AERO explicit instruction: teach the move, then fade; teacher-skills.md).
- Spoken/multimodal formats (the Year 8 staple: speeches, vlogs adapting voice to formal or informal audiences — reaserch.md, Persuasive formats Year 8 row) lean on *why this audience should care* more than on cited facts; a vivid, specific scenario counts as evidence.

## Modelling rule

Model every repair on a **different topic** (e.g. if the student wrote about uniforms, model on homework or phones), 1–3 sentences, then hand the student's own sentence back untouched.

## The Year 8 C→A lever

The single highest-leverage repair at this band: **turn one asserted reason into a developed argument — explain why it holds and why this audience should care.** That is the difference between listing opinions and building a case.
