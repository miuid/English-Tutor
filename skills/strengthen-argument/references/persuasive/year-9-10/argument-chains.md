# Argument chains (Year 9–10 persuasive)

Used by `strengthen-argument` to trace and repair the reasoning chain of a persuasive response. Anchored to the Year 9 persuasive expectation: viewpoints on social themes expressed through rhetorical strategies *and evidence* (reaserch.md — Persuasive formats, Year 9 row; Marking Criteria Year 9).

**Provenance note (Q-001):** Year 10 calibration is derived from the Year 9 elaborations, not verbatim QCAA text.

## The chain

**contention (framed against the social theme) → reason → elaboration → load-bearing evidence → link → (counterargument → rebuttal, when invited)**

At this band the chain must do more than hold — the evidence must be *load-bearing*: remove it and the reason collapses. Decorative reference to "research" or "experts" no longer counts as support.

## Break types, in priority order

| # | Break type | What it looks like at Year 9–10 | The repair to coach |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Viewpoint without substantiation** | A position on the social theme is stated, then asserted in louder words; reasons never detach from the viewpoint itself. | Generate ONE reason that could convince a sceptic, not just a believer. |
| 2 | **Reason without development** | Reasons are listed (often escalated correctly) but each is a headline, not an argument — no *why it holds*, no consequence chain. | Develop one reason into a because→which-means→so-the-audience-should sequence. |
| 3 | **Evidence not load-bearing** | Statistics or examples named but never tied back; "research shows" with nothing behind it; evidence that could be deleted without the argument noticing. | Make the evidence carry weight: specify it, then state explicitly what it proves and what follows if it's true. |
| 4 | **Missing or token rebuttal** | The format invites opposition (letter to the editor responding to critics, campaign pitch against a rival proposal, formal review weighing merit) and the strongest counterargument is ignored — or raised as a strawman and dismissed in half a line. | Steelman then rebut: state the opposing point at its strongest, then answer it with reasoning or evidence, not ridicule. |

## Priority rule

Choose the ONE focus break as the **lowest-numbered break type present**. Rhetorical polish cannot rescue an unsubstantiated viewpoint, so the chain always outranks devices (rhetorical control is `elevate-vocabulary`'s lane). A missing rebuttal is coached only when the format or task invites opposition — public-facing Year 9 formats (letters to the editor, campaign pitches, formal reviews; reaserch.md — Persuasive formats, Year 9 row) usually do; a one-sided speech usually doesn't.

## Band calibration

- A developed reason with relevant evidence is **sound, not excellent** at this band — the A-range gap is substantiation *plus* answering opposition. Name the sound links before coaching the broken one.
- The rebuttal is the signature move that separates Year 9–10 argument from Year 8 assertion — but never demand it where the task doesn't invite it.
- At Year 10, expect the counterargument to be the *strongest* available opposing point, not a convenient weak one (derived one band up per Q-001).

## Modelling rule

Model every repair on a **different topic**, 1–3 sentences, then hand the student's own text back untouched. A claim–counterclaim–rebuttal model on an unrelated social theme is the highest-value "I do" at this band.

## The Year 9–10 B→A lever

The single highest-leverage repair at this band: **make one piece of evidence load-bearing, or steelman-and-rebut the strongest counterargument the audience is already thinking.**
