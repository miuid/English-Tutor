# Expected behaviour for sample-02

The response must exhibit ALL of these (wording will vary):

**Traces the chain accurately.** Viewpoint is present and framed against the social theme; three reasons exist; the self-esteem reason has genuine development; "research shows… poor sleep" is evidence that is asserted, not load-bearing; the task explicitly invites opposition and the counterargument ("connects young people") is raised as a strawman and dismissed ("that is ridiculous") — a token rebuttal.

**Focuses on exactly ONE broken link.** By the priority rule, break type 3 (evidence not load-bearing — "research shows" with nothing behind it) outranks break type 4 here ONLY if type 3 is treated as present and type 4 absent; but the token dismissal means type 4 (missing/token rebuttal) is the defensible primary pick because the task explicitly responds to opposing claims. Either single focus is acceptable; flagging both as the focus is a fail.

**The rebuttal coaching, if chosen, must require a steelman**: state the "connection" claim at its strongest (e.g. isolated teens finding community), then answer it with reasoning — never ridicule. Must note the dismissal ("that is ridiculous") weakens the argument with a formal public audience.

**Names what works specifically** — e.g. the self-esteem reason IS developed (compare→harm), or the viewpoint is unmistakable from line one.

**Models the repair on a DIFFERENT topic** (e.g. a letter about junk-food advertising or phone bans), showing a claim–steelman–rebuttal or a made-load-bearing statistic in 1–3 sentences. Must NOT rewrite the student's social-media sentences.

**Hands the work back** with a targeted prompt and a concrete "I can…" criterion (e.g. "I can state the other side at its strongest and then answer it"). A rebuttal frame ("Critics say…, and it's true that…, but…") is appropriate scaffolding at Year 9.

**Does NOT comment on rhetorical devices, register, or vocabulary** — that is `elevate-vocabulary`'s lane.

## Fail conditions
- Rewrites the student's letter or supplies the missing rebuttal for the social-media argument (ghostwriting).
- Flags two or more broken links as the focus (unbounded).
- Treats the "research shows" line as load-bearing evidence (misread of the chain).
- Demands the student ridicule or dismiss the counterargument harder instead of steelmanning it.
- Drifts into vocabulary/register commentary (out of lane).
