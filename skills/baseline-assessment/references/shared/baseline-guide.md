# Baseline guide (shared — all text types and bands)

How to turn one 15-minute timed write into a day-0 baseline. Criterion-referenced: read against the QCAA A–E standard descriptors (reaserch.md — Evaluative Standards & Marking Rubrics), never against other students.

## Conditions

- One timed write, roughly 15 minutes, one sitting, no help. Short and imperfect is expected — the baseline is a *floor estimate*, explicitly revisable once real sessions accumulate.
- Read generously at the ceiling: award the highest level the writing genuinely evidences. When evidence is thin, take the lower level and note "not much evidence yet".
- Baseline prompts are low-stakes and open, e.g. analytical: "What makes a character in a story you know memorable — and how does the writer do it?"; persuasive: "Convince your reader of one change your school should make."; imaginative: "Write the opening of a story where a character finds something they weren't looking for."

## The five criteria per text type (use these names EXACTLY)

The baseline's per-criterion levels become the student's day-0 rubric scores and must line up with the loop's feedback rubric (skills/give-feedback/references/<text_type>/<band>/rubric.md), so the progress view trends day 0 → today on the same rows.

**Analytical (year-8 and year-9-10):**
1. Understanding of text / ideas
2. Analysis (how techniques create meaning)
3. Use of evidence
4. Structure & cohesion
5. Language & vocabulary

**Persuasive (year-8):**
1. Position & ideas
2. Argument & evidence
3. Audience & voice
4. Structure & cohesion
5. Language & vocabulary

**Persuasive (year-9-10):**
1. Viewpoint & ideas
2. Argument & substantiation
3. Rhetoric & audience
4. Structure & cohesion
5. Language & voice

**Imaginative (year-8):**
1. Story & tension
2. Character & setting
3. Showing & voice
4. Language & vocabulary
5. Structure & cohesion

**Imaginative (year-9-10):**
1. Structural control
2. Story & tension
3. Voice & tone
4. Character & setting
5. Language & imagery

Year 11-12 baselines use the same five names as year-9-10 for the text type (senior packs arrive with ISS-024/025; Q-001 marks senior descriptors as derived until official QCAA sources are imported).

## Band calibration (what the levels mean at each year level)

- **Year 8**: C is *sound* — a correct point plus evidence with thin explanation (analytical), a position with relevant reasons (persuasive), a clear complication developed and resolved (imaginative). Most Year 8 first writes sit C/D; that is a normal starting point, not a problem. (reaserch.md — Marking Criteria Year 8; A–E standard elaborations)
- **Year 9-10**: the bar shifts to critical analysis of representations and reader positioning (analytical), escalating substantiated viewpoint with deliberate rhetoric (persuasive), and purposeful structural/voice control (imaginative). A Year 9 write that would be a strong Year 8 C is still a Year 9 C — judge against the Year 9 descriptors. (reaserch.md — Year 9 marking criteria; A–E elaborations; Year 10 derived one band up per Q-001)
- **A/B from one short timed write is rare** — it requires purposeful, sustained control. Never award it for promise alone.

## Leverage ranking (which weakness attacks first)

When several criteria are low, rank by what lifts the standard fastest for the year level, not by what is easiest to spot:

1. **Meaning-making first.** Thin analysis/explanation (analytical), undeveloped reasons (persuasive), or a summarised/told story core (imaginative) outrank everything — they cap the grade no matter how polished the surface is. (reaserch.md — the Year 8 C→A lever: explain how the technique creates its effect)
2. **Structure second.** A missing thesis/contention/complication or disorganised paragraphs.
3. **Evidence/substantiation third.** Missing, dumped, or decorative support.
4. **Voice & audience fourth.** Register mismatches, indistinct narrator.
5. **Language & vocabulary last** among equals — flat word choice is the most visible but usually not the highest-leverage fix (the first student's "flat vocabulary" weakness is attacked *through* the loop, not by a word list on day one). (PRD §1 — MVP focus weaknesses)

## Weakness → starting focus map

Recommend exactly ONE starting focus (skill + text type); the loop's diagnose-errors router takes over from there.

| Dominant weakness | Starting focus |
|---|---|
| Thin analysis / missing explanation of effect (analytical) | `check-structure` on analytical (PEEL E1: explanation before/after the quote) |
| Missing/drifting thesis or disorganised paragraphs | `check-structure` on the session's text type |
| Flat, vague, repetitive word choice | `elevate-vocabulary` on the session's text type |
| Asserted reasons / decorative evidence / missing rebuttal (persuasive) | `strengthen-argument` on persuasive |
| Told-not-shown key moments, named emotions, POV drift (imaginative) | `craft-voice` on imaginative |
| Overall sound (C+ across the board) | `give-feedback`'s next-steps cycle on the session's text type — start the normal loop and let `diagnose-errors` route |

## Tone rules for the baseline report

- Warm, plain, forward-looking: "here's where we start and here's the plan", never a verdict.
- One specific strength, named from the actual writing. (HITS — feedback that anchors on evidence)
- The student hears the top lever only; the full ranking is for the plan/progress view.
- No mark talk with the student ("you got a D") — levels are recorded for the progress view; the conversation is about the next step.
