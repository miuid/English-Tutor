# Expected behaviour for sample-01

The response must exhibit ALL of these (wording will vary):

**Exact analytical criterion set.** The `## Per-criterion levels` section contains exactly five bullet lines named, in order: Understanding of text / ideas · Analysis (how techniques create meaning) · Use of evidence · Structure & cohesion · Language & vocabulary — each with a valid A–E level and a short evidence note. Any other criterion names (or a sixth criterion) is a fail.

**Levels match the evidence, read generously but never inflated.** This is a classic Year 8 C/D first write: sound literal understanding of why Harry is memorable (C-range understanding/ideas); assertion rather than analysis — "this shows he is brave" with no explanation of *how* the writing creates the effect (D-range analysis); one vague gesture at technique ("good describing words", the scar and glasses) with no real evidence integration (D-range evidence); functional intro/body/conclusion (C-range structure); clear but flat and repetitive wording ("really memorable", "really hard", "good friend") (C/D-range language). Nothing here evidences A/B on any criterion — awarding one is a fail.

**Ranked weaknesses in genuine leverage order, max three.** Analysis-before-everything: the top-ranked weakness must be thin analysis/explanation (the Year 8 C→A lever), ahead of evidence integration and flat vocabulary. Listing grammar, spelling, or more than three weaknesses is a fail.

**Exactly ONE starting focus with a plain reason.** Per the guide's map, thin analysis routes to `check-structure` on analytical writing. Recommending two skills, or a skill not in the map, is a fail.

**One specific strength, no mark talk.** Names a real move — e.g. he picked a genuine reason (bravery) and stayed on it, or the scar/glasses observation shows he's noticing what writers leave on the page. Must NOT quote A–E letters at the student, compare to other students, or frame the baseline as a grade.

## Fail conditions
- Wrong or renamed criteria, or anything other than exactly five level lines (breaks day-0 progress rows).
- Any A/B level, or levels that contradict the ranked weaknesses (e.g. analysis scored B while ranked the top weakness).
- More than 3 ranked weaknesses or more than 1 recommended focus (unbounded).
- Rewrites or "fixes" any of the student's sentences (ghostwriting — the baseline only reads).
- Verdict framing: "you scored", predictions, exam language, or empty praise ("great job!").
