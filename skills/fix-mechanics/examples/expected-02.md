# Expected behaviour for sample-02

The response must exhibit ALL of these (wording will vary):

**Coaches at most 2 patterns.** The strongest candidates:
1. **Dialogue punctuation** — the clear #1 at Year 9 imaginative: missing end punctuation inside quotes ("is anyone there"), missing comma before the closing quote ("I live here" he said), lowercase/uppercase handling around the quote. This is a single reusable rule-set and must be chosen.
2. **Apostrophes/homophones** — "Your not supposed to be here" (you're), "Mara didnt run" (didn't). Acceptable as the optional second pattern.

**Does NOT flag the deliberate fragment** "Nothing. Just wind and the slow rot of the stairs." — it is a purposeful craft choice (isolated dramatic beat), not an error. Flagging it as a fragment is a fail. (Praising it as deliberate is allowed and encouraged in the close.)

**Does NOT flag "a boys face"** as a separate third pattern — if apostrophes are chosen, this may be folded into that one pattern; it must not become pattern 3.

**Quotes the student's own sentence** for each chosen pattern and models each fix on an invented sentence on a different topic (e.g. a new line of dialogue about something else entirely). The student's own dialogue is never returned corrected.

**Hands the work back** with instance counts (e.g. "the dialogue rule shows up in three places — find them").

**Year 9–10 calibration**: the rule can be pitched more formally than Year 8 (e.g. naming "direct speech" conventions), and the close should notice something genuine — the atmosphere, the pacing, "pale as chalk", or the strong choice to withhold explanation.

## Fail conditions
- Corrects the student's dialogue for them (ghostwriting).
- Flags the deliberate fragment as an error.
- Coaches 3+ patterns or itemises every error.
- Drifts into voice/POV coaching (that's `craft-voice`) or plot structure (`check-structure`).
- Treats the colloquial dialogue itself ("Your not supposed to be here" as a character voice choice) as a register problem — the error is the apostrophe, not the voice.
