# QCAA task specifications (Year 9–10 imaginative)

Source: reaserch.md (QCAA Year 9 assessment techniques & conditions; approved assessment formats).

**Provenance note (Q-001):** Year 10 expectations are derived from the Year 9 elaborations, not verbatim QCAA text.

## Year 9 conditions

| Technique | Mode | Length | Time (assessment mode) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Short response | written / spoken / multimodal | written 100–300 words per item | within exam block |
| Extended response | written / spoken / multimodal | written **600–800 words**; spoken **3–5 min**; multimodal **4–6 min** | over an extended period |
| Examination | written, supervised | short 100–300/item; extended 600–800 | up to **90 min + 10 min planning** |

Scope at this band: structural experimentation, manipulating narrative voice, changing character points of view, and playing with tone (reaserch.md — Imaginative formats, Year 9 row).

## Imaginative task formats (Year 9)

Multi-text narratives · serialized vlogs · script transformations · monologues · digital stories (reaserch.md — Imaginative formats, Year 9 row).

## What this means for task design

- Name the **format** and the **experimental element** being practised (POV shift, non-linear time, tone play, monologue voice) — at this band experimentation is assessed, so the brief should make it deliberate, not accidental.
- Monologue and script tasks live or die on voice: the brief should define whose voice and who is being spoken to.
- Multi-text and serialized formats suit staged daily-loop practice (one instalment per day, voice held across instalments).
- Daily-loop practice pieces may be shorter than assessment length (one scene or one monologue beat ≈ 200–300 words); reserve 600–800 words for full/mock tasks.

## Copyright-safe stimulus rules

- Use original/generated scenarios, settings, and characters; script transformations must build on **public-domain** source texts (out-of-copyright works) or the student's own prior writing.
- Never paste copyrighted fiction, scripts, or vlog transcripts as stimulus; refer to a studied class text by name only and have the student supply their own excerpt.
