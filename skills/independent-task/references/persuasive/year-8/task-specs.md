# QCAA task specifications (Year 8 persuasive)

Source: reaserch.md (QCAA Year 8 assessment techniques & conditions; approved assessment formats).

## Year 8 conditions

| Technique | Mode | Length | Time (assessment mode) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Short response | written / spoken / multimodal | written 50–200 words per item (≤600 total); spoken ~1–2 min | within exam block |
| Extended response | written / spoken / multimodal | written **400–600 words**; spoken/multimodal **2–4 min** | over an extended period |
| Examination | written, supervised | short 50–200/item; extended 400–600 | up to **70 min + 10 min planning** |

Scope at this band: drawing on texts, developing ideas, and supporting viewpoints (reaserch.md — Extended Response Scope, Year 8).

## Persuasive task formats (Year 8)

Speeches · multimodal travel vlogs · vids · media reports. Focus: delivering multimodal spoken presentations that **adapt language and features of voice to suit formal or informal audiences** (reaserch.md — Persuasive formats, Year 8 row).

## What this means for task design

- Every persuasive task brief should name the **audience** (formal or informal) — audience adaptation is the assessed skill, not a nice-to-have.
- Spoken/multimodal formats dominate at this band: a written script for a 2–4 minute speech is a legitimate practice artefact.
- Daily-loop practice pieces may be shorter than assessment length (one developed argument paragraph ≈ 150–250 words); reserve 400–600 words for full/mock tasks.

## Copyright-safe stimulus rules

- Use original/generated scenarios and topics (school, community, environment, technology) — persuasive tasks need an issue, not a set text.
- Never paste in copyrighted articles, speeches, or advertisements as stimulus; refer to a public figure's speech by name and have the student supply their own copy if analysing it.
- Public-domain oratory (e.g. historical speeches out of copyright) may be used as stimulus where relevant.
